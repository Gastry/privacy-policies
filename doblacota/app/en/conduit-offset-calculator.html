30° offset with a 6-inch rise

With two opposite 30° bends, a 6-inch rise and a 4-inch centerline radius, the layout has 11.95 in of bend spacing and 1.51 in of shrink. Spacing is measured on the straight conduit between equivalent bend references. Shrink is the loss of reach along the original run.

To place the first mark, use the location of the offset and the reference for your bender. Check the manufacturer’s markings and test on a scrap of the same material to account for springback.

Offset formula with a bend radius

For rise H, radius R and angle θ in radians, the straight segment between arcs is L = [H − 2R(1 − cos θ)] ÷ sin θ. Bend spacing is L + Rθ, and shrink is L(1 − cos θ) + 2R(θ − sin θ). The curves require a rise of at least 2R(1 − cos θ).

Offset multiplier chart for radius zero

In the sharp-corner model, multiply rise by 1 ÷ sin θ for spacing and by tan(θ ÷ 2) for shrink. The table rounds these factors. A manufacturer’s bender chart, such as the Klein Tools guide, may use different rounding and references.
